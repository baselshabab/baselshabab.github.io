About me

Investigating materials that protect subsurface energy systems

I am a Ph.D. student in Mining & Minerals Engineering at Virginia Tech, specializing in wellbore integrity for underground hydrogen storage and the performance of subsurface cementitious materials under energy-storage conditions.

My doctoral research focuses on the mechanical, microstructural, and chemical evolution of Class H wellbore cement under high-pressure hydrogen exposure using field-retrieved formation waters. I am particularly interested in cement degradation, pore-network evolution, hydrogen transport pathways, and sealing performance in legacy wells associated with depleted gas reservoirs.

My work integrates mechanical testing and transport measurements with advanced characterization techniques including SEM-EDS, XRD, XRF, TGA, ICP-MS, and micro-CT to connect material-scale changes with long-term wellbore performance.

Beyond my primary doctoral research, I have worked on fracture-based characterization of cemented paste backfill and am increasingly interested in integrating computational modeling with experimental observations to study subsurface material behavior across scales.

I also have applied experience in CO₂ storage techno-economics through the SEG Evolve Carbon Solutions program, where I contributed to the evaluation of offshore carbon-storage opportunities in offshore Texas.

I am interested in research and engineering roles where experimental geomechanics, subsurface materials characterization, and data-driven analysis can support hydrogen storage, well integrity, and broader energy-transition technologies.

Research project · Spring 2025 Virginia Tech

Fracture-Based Strength Characterization of Cemented Paste Backfill

Contributed to a collaborative project developing a hydraulic-fracturing method to estimate the true in-situ strength of placed cemented paste backfill, addressing limitations of conventional strength tests that do not fully capture in-place behavior.

Objective

Support the development and validation of a fracturing-based tool that uses fracture initiation pressure (FIP) to estimate the in-situ tensile strength of cemented paste backfill as an alternative to traditional strength testing.

Contribution

Performed S- and P-wave velocity measurements and evaluated elastic properties of CPB samples, including Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, and tensile strength, to support benchmarking of fracture initiation pressure against conventional strength measures such as UCS, BTS, and Mode-I fracture toughness (KIC).

Impact

Findings supported more realistic estimates of in-situ backfill strength for mine-design applications and contributed to a peer-reviewed publication in Mining, Metallurgy & Exploration (Frimpong et al., 2025).

  • Cemented paste backfill
  • Ultrasonic velocity
  • Elastic properties
  • Mine design
  • Geomechanics

Journal Article

Fracture initiation pressure as a measure of cemented paste backfill strength

James A Frimpong, Basel Ahmad Shabab, Rohit Pandey, Snehamoy Chatterjee, Gabriel Walton, Alexander S Brand

Mining, Metallurgy & Exploration, 42, 3, 1305-1323

This laboratory-scale study presents the development and validation of a hydraulic fracturing technique to directly measure the tensile strength of cemented paste backfill (CPB), providing an alternative to traditional strength testing methods. Fracture initiation pressure (FIP) was used as the primary measure of CPB strength. Experimental results were compared with traditional benchmark measures such as uniaxial compressive strength (UCS), Brazilian tensile strength (BTS), and critical Mode-I fracture toughness (KIc). Regression analysis of experimental results revealed a strong linear relationship between FIP and these benchmark strength measures, indicating that FIP can be used as a reliable predictor of CPB strength.

Research Poster

Site-scale evaluation of geologic carbon storage at the Matagorda Island leasing area, offshore Texas

Jarely Mendez, Isabella Andrade, Augustine J Effiong, Emmanuel Paul Irumhe, Benjamin O Lawson, Basel A Shabab, Ryan Pollyea

SPE/AAPG/SEG Carbon, Capture, Utilization, and Storage Conference and Exhibition, Houston, Texas, USA, March 2025.

This study evaluates the CO₂ storage potential of Miocene saline reservoirs in the Matagorda Island region offshore Texas using 3D seismic data, well logs, static geomodeling, and dynamic simulation. Results indicate that extensive reservoir units and structural traps associated with the Corsair and Clemente-Thomas faults could support large-scale CCS development, with the potential to store millions of tonnes of CO₂ and serve nearby Gulf Coast industrial hubs.

Oral Presentation

Multiscale Assessment of Wellbore Cement Integrity in Underground Hydrogen Storage

Presented by Basel Ahmad Shabab

60th U.S. Rock Mechanics/Geomechanics Symposium, Tucson, Arizona, June 2026

This study evaluates the integrity of Class H wellbore cement under underground hydrogen storage conditions using field-derived formation fluids. Cement specimens exposed to pressurized hydrogen or helium showed reduced porosity and permeability, increased tensile strength, and microstructural densification, indicating limited hydrogen transport and sustained sealing performance under UHS-representative conditions.
