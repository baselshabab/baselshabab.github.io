About me

Investigating materials that protect subsurface energy systems

I am a Ph.D. student and Graduate Research Assistant in Mining & Minerals Engineering at Virginia Tech. My doctoral research examines wellbore integrity for underground hydrogen storage, focusing on how Class H cement evolves under high-pressure hydrogen exposure with field-retrieved formation waters and what those changes mean for sealing performance in legacy wells in depleted gas reservoirs.

My background also includes fracture-based characterization of cemented paste backfill and CO₂ storage techno-economics through SEG Evolve. I am interested in connecting experimental geomechanics and materials characterization with computational modeling and data analysis, and in research and engineering opportunities in industry and national laboratories that support subsurface energy storage and well integrity.

Research

Research questions

Questions connecting material-scale behavior with the reliability of underground energy storage and mining systems.

Primary focus

Wellbore Integrity for Underground Hydrogen Storage

How do high-pressure hydrogen and formation-water chemistry change Class H cement, and what do those changes imply for legacy-well sealing?

I investigate degradation, pore-network evolution, and potential hydrogen migration pathways under storage conditions, including cyclic exposure. A central question is how changes within cement and at rock–cement interfaces affect transport and mechanical response.

Cement degradation Pore network evolution Rock–cement interfaces Hydrogen transport pathways Legacy-well sealing
Secondary

Mechanical Behavior of Cemented Paste Backfill

Can fracture initiation pressure provide a useful measure of cemented paste backfill strength, and how does it compare with conventional laboratory strength measurements when assessing in-place behavior?

Emerging

Computational Modeling + Experimental Characterization

How can experiment-informed models connect pore-scale observations with transport and mechanical response across scales? This is an emerging research interest aimed at interpreting long-term subsurface material performance.

Journal Article

Fracture initiation pressure as a measure of cemented paste backfill strength

James A Frimpong, Basel Ahmad Shabab, Rohit Pandey, Snehamoy Chatterjee, Gabriel Walton, Alexander S Brand

Mining, Metallurgy & Exploration, 42, 3, 1305-1323

This laboratory-scale study presents the development and validation of a hydraulic fracturing technique to directly measure the tensile strength of cemented paste backfill (CPB), providing an alternative to traditional strength testing methods. Fracture initiation pressure (FIP) was used as the primary measure of CPB strength. Experimental results were compared with traditional benchmark measures such as uniaxial compressive strength (UCS), Brazilian tensile strength (BTS), and critical Mode-I fracture toughness (KIc). Regression analysis of experimental results revealed a strong linear relationship between FIP and these benchmark strength measures, indicating that FIP can be used as a reliable predictor of CPB strength.

Research Poster

Site-scale evaluation of geologic carbon storage at the Matagorda Island leasing area, offshore Texas

Jarely Mendez, Isabella Andrade, Augustine J Effiong, Emmanuel Paul Irumhe, Benjamin O Lawson, Basel A Shabab, Ryan Pollyea

SPE/AAPG/SEG Carbon, Capture, Utilization, and Storage Conference and Exhibition, Houston, Texas, USA, March 2025.

This study evaluates the CO₂ storage potential of Miocene saline reservoirs in the Matagorda Island region offshore Texas using 3D seismic data, well logs, static geomodeling, and dynamic simulation. Results indicate that extensive reservoir units and structural traps associated with the Corsair and Clemente-Thomas faults could support large-scale CCS development, with the potential to store millions of tonnes of CO₂ and serve nearby Gulf Coast industrial hubs.

Oral Presentation

Multiscale Assessment of Wellbore Cement Integrity in Underground Hydrogen Storage

Presented by Basel Ahmad Shabab

60th U.S. Rock Mechanics/Geomechanics Symposium, Tucson, Arizona, June 2026

This study evaluates the integrity of Class H wellbore cement under underground hydrogen storage conditions using field-derived formation fluids. Cement specimens exposed to pressurized hydrogen or helium showed reduced porosity and permeability, increased tensile strength, and microstructural densification, indicating limited hydrogen transport and sustained sealing performance under UHS-representative conditions.

Current doctoral research Virginia Tech

Class H Wellbore Cement Performance Under High-Pressure Hydrogen Exposure

Legacy wells may provide hydrogen migration pathways if their cement seals change under storage conditions. My doctoral work examines this problem using Class H cement and field-retrieved formation waters.

My contribution

I characterize mechanical, microstructural, chemical, and transport changes in exposed cement, combining strength and transport measurements with imaging and mineralogical analysis.

Experimental context

In the published study, specimens were cured for 45 days at 50 °C, then exposed for 75 days at 1000 psia to hydrogen or helium in combination with two field-derived formation waters.

Supported findings

Reported specimens showed reduced porosity and permeability, increased tensile strength, and microstructural densification. These laboratory observations inform sealing behavior under the tested conditions; they do not establish field-scale or lifetime performance.

Fracture-Based Strength Characterization of Cemented Paste Backfill

Conventional laboratory strength tests do not fully capture the behavior of placed cemented paste backfill. This collaborative, laboratory-scale study evaluated hydraulic fracturing as an alternative way to characterize strength.

My contribution

I performed S- and P-wave velocity measurements and evaluated elastic properties, including Young’s modulus and Poisson’s ratio, and tensile strength to support comparisons with fracture initiation pressure (FIP).

Supported findings

The team compared FIP with uniaxial compressive strength, Brazilian tensile strength, and critical Mode-I fracture toughness. Regression analysis showed strong linear relationships within the experimental dataset.

Research deliverable

The work contributed to Frimpong et al. (2025) in Mining, Metallurgy & Exploration. It supports further evaluation of fracture-based strength estimates for mine design; the reported validation was at laboratory scale.

SEG Evolve Virtual Internship Offshore Texas

Economic Analysis of Offshore CO₂ Sequestration Projects

Potential CO₂ storage development offshore Texas requires both subsurface characterization and an assessment of project economics. Through SEG Evolve, I worked with a six-member interdisciplinary team on the Matagorda Island leasing area.

My contribution

I led techno-economic analysis for a project scenario of approximately 15 Mt over a 15-year injection period, built the economic model and risk analysis, developed a measurement, monitoring, and verification (MMV) plan, and identified pipeline routing from source to sink.

Team assessment

The team integrated 3D seismic data, well logs, static geomodeling, and dynamic simulation to evaluate Miocene saline reservoirs and structural traps associated with the Corsair and Clemente-Thomas faults.

Project deliverables

The economic analysis, MMV plan, and routing assessment supported the site-scale evaluation presented in a March 2025 CCUS conference poster. Storage estimates describe evaluated potential, rather than demonstrated injection performance.
